What is a stock screener (stock scanner)?

A stock screener, also called a stock scanner, applies fixed technical rules to every listed stock and shows only the stocks that match. This scanner reads the official NSE Bhavcopy after each session and runs 28 daily screens, a custom screener, 45+ candlestick patterns, chart-pattern and breakout checks, market breadth, sector strength and an ETF screener across about 2,500 EQ‑series stocks and ETFs, so you can review the whole market in minutes instead of scrolling through charts one by one.

Market breadth and the advance‑decline ratio

The advance‑decline ratio is the number of stocks that closed higher divided by the number that closed lower. A reading above 1 means more stocks rose than fell. Breadth matters because an index can rise on a handful of large stocks while most shares fall.

The breadth strip at the top of the scanner combines four measures: advances versus declines, the share of liquid stocks above their 50‑day and 200‑day averages, new 52‑week highs versus lows, and Nifty’s own position (tracked through NIFTYBEES) against its 50- and 200‑day averages and its 20‑session return. These are combined into a simple Bullish, Mixed or Bearish reading, with the reasons listed in the Market & sectors tab and a 10‑session history of advances and declines. Rule-based setups: how they are built

Setups combine several conditions at once, which a single screen cannot. Each one lists a trigger (the price that must trade for the setup to count), an invalidation level (where the technical premise no longer holds) and a reference target, together with a 0–100 score that ranks setups by how many quality checks they pass. The score is a ranking aid, not a probability.

Candlestick patterns

A candlestick shows one session’s open, high, low and close. The body covers the open-to-close range and the wicks show the full high-to-low range. When candles form recognisable shapes, alone or across two to five sessions, they are called candlestick patterns. The scanner checks 45+ of them every session. See today’s candlestick patterns, or jump to bullish and bearish candlestick patterns.

How the scanner keeps patterns meaningful

A reversal pattern only means something if there was a trend to reverse. The scanner measures the trend before the pattern started, using the first candle’s close against its own 20‑session average and the 5‑session slope, and reports a Hammer only after a decline and a Shooting Star only after an advance. Indecision candles such as Doji and Spinning Top are reported only when the stock is at least 3% away from its 20‑day average, where indecision is more informative.

Chart patterns and breakouts

Chart patterns are found from swing highs and lows over up to 30 sessions, with the swing size scaled to each stock’s own volatility (average true range). A pattern is reported only on the session its neckline or range is crossed, so the list shows new events rather than old ones.

Double top
Two similar peaks after an advance, with a close below the low between them.
Double bottom
Two similar troughs after a decline, with a close above the high between them.
Head and shoulders
Three peaks with the middle one highest and a close below the neckline. The inverse version is the mirror image.
20‑day breakout / breakdown
First close beyond the previous 20‑session high or low, on at least 1.1 times average volume.

Trend stage (S1–S4): Weinstein-style stage analysis

Stan Weinstein’s stage analysis divides a stock’s cycle into four stages. The original method uses a 30‑week moving average; this scanner uses an end‑of‑day version built from the 20-, 50- and 200‑day averages.

S1: Base

The 50‑day average is at or below the 200‑day, and price has recovered above the 50‑day. Often a basing phase after a decline.

S2: Uptrend

Price above the 20- and 50‑day averages, with the 50‑day above the 200‑day. S2 pullback: price has dipped below the 20‑day but the structure is intact.

S3: Top

The 20‑day average has turned below the 50‑day while price is still above the 200‑day. Momentum is fading.

S4: Downtrend

Price below both the 50- and 200‑day averages.

Historically, bullish patterns in S2 and bearish patterns in S4 have tended to be more consistent than patterns that run against the stage. Newly listed stocks without enough history are shown as unclear rather than given a stage.

Near support

The Near support badge appears when the close is between 0.5% below and 2% above the 50‑day average, the 200‑day average or the 52‑week low. A level that price is trading below acts as resistance, so it does not count. Support is a technical reference, not a guaranteed floor.

Track record, hit rate and methodology

  • Track record: every setup is tracked hypothetically. It counts only if its trigger traded within the allowed window. If the invalidation level and the reference target were touched on the same day, the invalidation is assumed to have come first. Gaps fill at the open, estimated costs are deducted (about 0.25% per round trip, 0.06% for one-session setups), and setups spanning a split or bonus are cancelled. Setups whose trigger never traded are counted separately. See the track record
  • Pattern hit rate: the share of past signals where the close moved in the pattern’s direction after 1, 3, 5 or 10 sessions. Patterns with fewer than 30 signals are faded, because the percentage is not yet reliable. See candlestick pattern hit rates
  • Data: prices, volume and delivery come from the official NSE Bhavcopy, published after market hours. Moving averages and 52‑week high and low come from Google Finance. If the Bhavcopy is not yet available, data is marked provisional.
  • Timing: updated every trading evening, usually between 6:30 PM and 8:00 PM IST. This is not a live or intraday feed.
  • Corporate actions: splits and bonuses are detected from the Bhavcopy’s adjusted previous close, older prices are adjusted, and affected stocks are kept out of setups for a few sessions.

The session in which the 50‑day moving average rises above the 200‑day moving average; the opposite is a death cross.
All-time high
The highest price a stock has traded at since listing, adjusted for splits and bonuses.
Bulk deal
A trade by one client of more than 0.5% of a company’s listed shares in one session, disclosed by NSE.
Trigger
The price that must trade for a setup to count as active in the track record.
Invalidation level
The price at which the setup’s technical premise no longer holds; used as the stop level in the track record.
Reference target
A technical reference level, usually 2 to 2.5 times the trigger-to-invalidation distance, or a nearby swing level when one exists.

Frequently asked questions

What is a stock screener (stock scanner)?

A stock screener, also called a stock scanner, applies fixed rules to every listed stock and returns only the ones that match, for example stocks at a 52‑week high, stocks trading on unusually high volume, or stocks that formed a candlestick pattern. MNCL’s screener for NSE stocks runs these screens on official end‑of‑day data for about 2,500 EQ‑series stocks after every trading session.

Is this stock screener free, and is it live?

It is free to use. It is an end‑of‑day scanner, not a live one: it is updated each trading evening after NSE publishes the Bhavcopy, usually between 6:30 PM and 8:00 PM IST, and shows the most recently completed session.

How is the advance‑decline ratio calculated?

The advance‑decline ratio is the number of stocks that closed higher divided by the number that closed lower in the same session. The scanner counts liquid NSE stocks (daily turnover of at least ₹5 lakh). A ratio above 1 means more stocks rose than fell; below 1 means more fell than rose.

How are 52‑week high stocks screened?

A stock appears in the 52‑week high screen when its high for the session reached its 52‑week high and it closed within 3% of that level. Only stocks with daily turnover of at least ₹1 crore are included.

What are volume shockers?

Volume shockers are stocks whose traded volume in the latest session was at least 2.5 times their average daily volume over the previous 10 sessions, measured from NSE Bhavcopy data. A volume spike shows unusual activity; it does not show whether that activity was buying or selling.

What does a high delivery percentage mean?

Delivery percentage is the share of traded quantity that was settled by delivery into demat accounts rather than squared off within the day. The high delivery screen lists stocks with delivery of 60% or more that is also at least 25% above the stock’s own 20‑session average.

What is an NR7 stock?

NR7 means narrow range 7: the stock’s high‑low range in the latest session was smaller than in each of the previous six sessions. It marks a contraction in volatility, which traders watch because ranges tend to expand afterwards, in either direction.

What does RSI oversold mean?

RSI, the Relative Strength Index, measures the speed of recent price changes on a 0 to 100 scale. The scanner uses a 14‑session RSI and lists stocks at 30 or below as oversold and 70 or above as overbought. These readings describe momentum, not value, and strong trends can stay oversold or overbought for long periods.

Which candlestick and chart patterns does the scanner detect?

It checks 45+ candlestick patterns, including Hammer, Engulfing, Harami, Morning and Evening Star, Three White Soldiers, Doji and Marubozu, and chart patterns including Double Top, Double Bottom, Head and Shoulders, Inverse Head and Shoulders and 20‑day breakouts and breakdowns. Reversal patterns are only reported when the trend before the pattern matches the pattern’s definition.

How are breakout stocks identified?

A 20‑day breakout is the first close above the highest high of the previous 20 sessions, on at least 1.2 times average volume in the screen and 1.1 times in the pattern list. A breakdown is the first close below the lowest low of the previous 20 sessions. Only the first day of the move is shown, so the list contains fresh breakouts rather than stocks that broke out earlier.

How are all-time high stocks identified?

A stock appears in the all-time high screen when its high for the session reached the highest price on record and it closed within 3% of that level. All-time values come from Google Finance price history since 2000 and are raised or lowered every session from the official NSE Bhavcopy, adjusted for splits and bonuses.

What is a golden crossover?

A golden crossover (golden cross) is the session in which a stock’s 50‑day moving average rises above its 200‑day moving average. A death cross is the opposite. Both are lagging signals: they confirm a trend change that has already happened in price, and are often followed by sideways trade.

What is the momentum score?

The momentum score ranks every liquid NSE stock from 0 to 100 using five inputs: the 20‑session return (30% weight), the 5‑session return (15%), the trend stage (25%), how close the price is to its 52‑week high (20%) and volume against the 10‑day average (10%). A high score means strong recent relative performance. It is a ranking, not a rating or a forecast.

Can I build my own stock screen?

Yes. The Build a screen tab combines filters on price, change, 5- and 20‑session return, volume, delivery percentage, RSI, 50- and 200‑day averages, distance from the 52‑week and all-time high, momentum score, relative strength against Nifty, sector, industry, trend stage, candlestick pattern and moving-average crossovers. Every filter is kept in the page address, so a screen can be bookmarked or shared, and screens can be saved in your browser.

How are ETFs grouped?

ETFs are recognised from their name and grouped by what they hold: gold, silver, debt and liquid, international, smart beta and factor, banking and financial, PSU and CPSE, IT, sectoral, thematic, midcap and smallcap, and broad Nifty or Sensex funds. They are shown in their own ETF section and excluded from stock screens and market breadth. ETF prices on the exchange can differ from the fund’s NAV.

What are bulk deals and block deals?

A bulk deal is a trade in which one client buys or sells more than 0.5% of a company’s listed shares in a single session. A block deal is a single large trade of at least ₹10 crore done in the exchange’s separate block-deal window. NSE discloses both after the session, with the client name, quantity and price. The scanner lists the last 45 days.

What is a MACD crossover and a Supertrend signal?

MACD is the difference between the 12- and 26‑session exponential moving averages; a bullish crossover is the session in which it rises above its own 9‑session average (the signal line). Supertrend (10, 3) is a trailing line three average true ranges away from the price; it flips up when the close moves above it and down when the close falls below it. Both describe changes in momentum that have already happened and give many false signals in sideways markets.

Can I add my own indicator to the chart?

Yes. Besides the built-in indicators, the chart accepts a formula using open, high, low, close and volume with functions such as sma, ema, rsi, atr, highest, lowest and stdev, for example ema(close,20) - ema(close,50). The formula is drawn on the price or in its own pane and is saved in your browser.

Are the setups recommendations to buy or sell?

No. Setups are the output of automated technical rules applied to end‑of‑day data. They are not recommendations to buy, sell or hold any security, and the trigger, invalidation and reference levels are technical reference points only. Please consult a SEBI-registered investment adviser or research analyst before making investment decisions.

How is the track record calculated?

Every setup is tracked hypothetically. It counts only if its trigger level actually traded within the allowed window. If the invalidation level and the reference target were both touched on the same day, the invalidation is assumed to have come first. Gaps fill at the opening price, estimated costs are deducted, and setups that span a split or bonus are cancelled. Past outcomes do not indicate future results.

What is an ex-dividend date and who gets the dividend?

The ex-dividend date is the first session in which a share trades without the right to the declared dividend. With India’s T+1 settlement the record date is usually the same day, so only investors holding the shares at the close of the session before the ex-date receive the dividend. The share price usually opens lower by about the dividend amount on the ex-date. The Corporate actions screens list upcoming and recent ex-dates from NSE’s corporate actions list.

How do a stock split and a bonus issue affect the share price?

Both increase the number of shares and reduce the price in the same proportion on the ex-date, so the value of a holding does not change. A 1:1 bonus doubles the shares and roughly halves the price; a split from ₹10 to ₹1 face value gives ten shares for each one at about a tenth of the price. Charts, moving averages and 52‑week highs on this page are adjusted for splits and bonuses so the change does not look like a fall.

What is dividend yield?

Dividend yield is the dividend per share paid over the last 12 months divided by the current share price. A 2% yield means ₹2 of dividends a year for every ₹100 of share price. It is based on past dividends: a high yield can come from a one-off special dividend or a fall in the share price, and future dividends are not assured.

Where does the data come from?

Prices, volume and delivery percentage come from the official NSE Bhavcopy (the full Bhavcopy with delivery data). Index values come from NSE’s daily index closing file and bulk and block deals from NSE’s daily deal disclosures. Moving averages, 52‑week and all-time highs and lows come from Google Finance history and are updated from the Bhavcopy. On days when the Bhavcopy is not yet available, prices are marked provisional until it is loaded.
